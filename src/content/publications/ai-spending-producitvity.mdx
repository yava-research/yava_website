### Productivity

Businesses using AI
- they sell Goods and services produced with AI assistance
- but we need to ask if they can retain the productivity gains?

AI may increase productivity by reducing the time and resources required to complete a task. But productivity gains do not automatically translate into higher profits for the company using the technology. If every competitor gains access to similar tools, prices may fall and customers may capture much of the benefit. Companies with differentiated data, customer relationships, distribution, or pricing power may be better positioned to retain a share of the gains.

A simple example
| | Before AI | After AI: company retains savings | After AI: savings passed to customers |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Revenue | $100 | $100 | $90 |
| Operating costs | $80 | $70 | $70 |
| **Operating profit** | **$20** | **$30** | **$20** |


### Beneficiaries of using cheaper AI
These businesses may benefit after AI becomes affordable and useful:
- Companies with labor-intensive workflows that can be improved.
- Software businesses with distribution and embedded customer relationships.
- Businesses with proprietary data that can make AI products more valuable.
- Firms able to deliver a better product at lower cost and retain part of the savings.

> The investment question is:
> - Can the business keep the productivity gains, or will they be competed away?
