The useful test for an AI agent is simple: can it finish a task for me, safely, with less effort than doing it myself? Searching is only the beginning. An agent also has to navigate services, understand constraints, obtain permission and recover when something goes wrong. That makes distribution, payments and access to services as important as the model itself.

## From answers to actions

An assistant that summarizes restaurants is useful; one that finds a meal within a budget, checks delivery time and submits an approved order could be more valuable. The difference is responsibility. An incorrect answer wastes time, while an incorrect purchase can cost money or expose private information. I expect early adoption to favor narrow tasks with clear limits, visible prices and a final confirmation.

## Tencent and the distribution advantage

![Tencent's AI agent interface](/publications/tencent_ai_agents.jpeg)

Tencent's October 2026 results describe Xiaowei as a small-scale agent prototype inside Weixin, using Mini Programs to help users discover products, make purchase decisions and place orders. An existing ecosystem gives Tencent a potential distribution advantage: users and merchants already meet inside the same application. This is a prototype, however, not proof that autonomous transactions have reached scale. [Tencent results](https://www.tencent.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/c700_Joshua-PS-Publication-Announcement.pdf).

The metrics I would watch are repeat use, completed transactions, error and refund rates, and how often a user must intervene. A large installed base can speed experimentation, but trust and merchant participation determine whether the feature becomes a habit.

## Agentic payments

An agent needs an authorized way to pay, a spending limit and a record of what the user approved. Visa is developing tools for agentic commerce, while payment credentials and network rules can vary by implementation. [Visa, agentic commerce](https://corporate.visa.com/en/solutions/acceptance/agentic-commerce.html). It would be premature to assume every agent uses a platform-issued commercial card or that one interchange spread describes the whole market.

The economic question is who captures the value of a transaction: the agent, merchant, issuer, payment network or another intermediary. Merchants will weigh any new fees against incremental sales, fraud losses and customer acquisition costs. Users need clear confirmation, dispute rights and a way to revoke authority. The winning payment flow must work for all three sides.

## Food delivery and travel

Food delivery is a natural test case: preferences recur, inventories change and the order can be checked before payment. The agent still needs to handle substitutions, fees, allergies and delivery addresses. A useful measure is the share of orders completed accurately without costly support, not just the number of prompts.

Travel is harder. A flight choice involves baggage, connections, fare rules, changes and identity information. In November 2026 United restricted personal AI agents from booking or modifying tickets through its systems; Delta said it was evaluating external agents. [Fast Company, November 11, 2026](https://www.fastcompany.com/91640548/united-airlines-just-made-a-big-change-to-flight-bookings-heres-why-delta-and-american-arent-following). Access is therefore a business and policy question as well as a technical one. An agent that can compare options but cannot book or service a ticket has only solved part of the problem.

## The compute investment question

My long-term interest in Arm is an indirect way to follow growth in computing. Arm licenses processor designs and earns royalties on chips incorporating its technology; it reported fiscal 2026 revenue of USD 4.92 billion. [Arm annual report](https://investors.arm.com/static-files/29742a1f-33e5-4320-9a45-4079efcdb321). Agents may increase demand for the CPUs that coordinate software and infrastructure, alongside accelerators that run models, but more agent activity does not translate mechanically into Arm revenue.

The bull case is that growing cloud workloads and adoption of Arm-based designs lift royalty revenue. The bear case is that competition, customer concentration, spending on new products and valuation absorb the benefit. I would watch data-center royalties, licensing quality and operating costs rather than treat the existence of agents as sufficient evidence for a long position. Arm describes these and other risks in its [September 2026 filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1973239/000197323926000142/arm-20260930.htm).

## What would change my mind

I would become more optimistic if independent evidence showed repeat use, reliable task completion and transaction economics that work for merchants as well as platforms. I would become less optimistic if errors, access restrictions or the cost of supervision kept agents confined to demonstrations. For now, the opportunity is real, but widespread autonomous commerce remains a hypothesis to test.
