## What portable alpha solves

Normally, if you have \$100 and want exposure to both international equities and a diversifying global macro or managed futures strategy, traditional portfolio construction forces a zero-sum trade-off:

- **Traditional unstacked split (50/50):** Put \$50 into international stocks and \$50 into managed futures. You get 50% of each, cutting volatility and drawdowns, but surrendering half of your stock exposure creates a structural compounding drag during bull markets.
- **Return-stacked portable alpha (~200% gross exposure):** Use capital-efficient futures, swaps, or margin collateral so each \$100 of equity capital supports roughly \$100 of international stock exposure *plus* \$100 of multi-asset macro and trend-following exposure.

![Diagram contrasting traditional cash-funded liquid alternatives with portable alpha return stacking that layers an independent alpha strategy on top of core equity market exposure](/publications/portable-alpha.svg)

In institutional practice—seen in BlackRock's portable alpha framework and return-stacked ETFs such as `RSIT` (Return Stacked International Stocks & Managed Futures)—the architecture separates beta from alpha into two independent sleeves:

1. **Core beta sleeve (developed ex-U.S. equities):** Captures the long-term growth of developed international equity markets outside the United States (tracking benchmarks like MSCI EAFE, proxied by `EFA`) through a capital-efficient mix of physical securities, underlying ETFs, and international equity index futures. Keeping the equity core intact removes the need to sell stocks to fund alternatives.
2. **Alpha overlay sleeve (global macro and systematic trend):** Layers rules-based long and short derivatives positions across four liquid global asset classes—**Equities, Rates/Bonds, Currencies (FX), and Commodities**—based on price trends, macro regimes, carry, and cross-asset dispersion.

To capture the managed futures category average while avoiding hedge-fund performance fees and single-manager risk, live implementations such as `RSIT` blend a **Bottom-Up approach (70%)**—running systematic multi-asset models directly on historical market data—with a **Top-Down approach (30%)** that regresses recent CTA manager returns onto liquid futures to adapt to current industry positioning.

## Why return stacking works and the catch

The mathematical case for portable alpha rests on **uncorrelated risk addition**. Layering a 10%-volatility alpha sleeve with near-zero stock correlation ($\rho \approx 0$) onto a 20%-volatility equity core increases total portfolio volatility far less than linearly:

$$
\sigma_{\text{stacked}} = \sqrt{\sigma_{\text{beta}}^2 + \sigma_{\text{alpha}}^2 + 2 \rho \sigma_{\text{beta}} \sigma_{\text{alpha}}} \approx \sqrt{20\%^2 + 10\%^2} \approx 22.4\%
$$

In exchange for roughly **+2 percentage points** of incremental volatility, the investor keeps full equity participation and adds an independent return stream designed to perform during inflation shocks and rate-hiking cycles when stocks and bonds sell off together.

The catch is equally mechanical. Because you hold roughly \$2 of economic exposure for every \$1 of capital, **leverage amplifies both gains and losses**: headline volatility is higher than unleveraged stocks alone, implied equity futures financing creates a persistent hurdle rate, and both sleeves can lose money simultaneously during sharp liquidity crunches or V-shaped macro reversals.

## Prototyping the strategy with Yahoo Finance

Testing signal logic, macro-versus-trend weights, and multi-asset behavior on real historical data requires fixing three common traps that appear in quick `yfinance` prototypes:

1. **Continuous futures roll gaps and calendar mismatch:** Querying raw Yahoo continuous futures (`ES=F`, `ZN=F`, `6E=F`, `CL=F`) introduces unadjusted contract roll jumps, April 2020 negative crude oil prints, and non-NYSE holiday bars that inject artificial zero-return days when aligned to equity ETFs (`EFA`/`VXUS`).
2. **Gross-notional normalization versus risk budgeting:** Normalizing raw weights so $\sum_i \lvert w_i \rvert = 1.0$ lets low-volatility short-term bonds (`SHY`/`ZT=F`) absorb most of the notional budget while compressing realized overlay volatility well below a 10%–12% target.
3. **Unfunded futures excess-return accounting:** When proxying futures with total-return ETFs, subtracting the daily 1–3 month Treasury bill return (`BIL`) is mandatory so long and short positions reflect true unfunded futures excess returns rather than embedded cash yields.

### Unfunded futures accounting with ETF proxies

We assemble a symmetric **16-instrument, 4-asset-class universe** of liquid total-return ETFs (four contracts per class) aligned strictly to **4,809 NYSE equity trading days** (October 11, 2007 – November 20, 2026; **4,541 evaluation days** from November 3, 2008 after a 252-day warm-up) and convert each return to an unfunded futures excess return:

$$
r^{\text{xs}}_{i,t} = R^{\text{ETF}}_{i,t} - R_{\text{BIL}, t}
$$

This adjustment is mathematically exact across every asset class in our universe:

- **Physical equity, bond, and bullion ETFs** (`SPY`, `VGK`, `EWJ`, `EEM`, `SHY`, `IEF`, `TLT`, `BWX`, `GLD`, `SLV`): Subtracting $R_{\text{BIL}, t}$ strips out the risk-free financing rate embedded in forward/futures contracts.
- **Currency deposit ETFs** (`FXE`, `FXY`, `FXB`, `FXA`): Holding foreign cash earns spot FX moves plus foreign deposit rates; subtracting USD T-bills recovers spot return plus $r_{\text{foreign}} - r_{\text{USD}}$, matching currency futures via covered interest parity.
- **Collateralized commodity futures ETFs** (`USO`, `DBA`): These funds earn futures excess return *plus* T-bill collateral yield; subtracting $R_{\text{BIL}, t}$ isolates pure unfunded commodity futures return.

When the stacked portfolio holds \$100 of T-bill collateral (earning $R_{\text{BIL}, t}$ once), funds \$100 of `EFA` exposure through futures (paying $R_{\text{BIL}, t} + s_{\text{fin}}$), and posts remaining T-bills as margin for the unfunded overlay, the T-bill yield cancels cleanly:

$$
R_{\text{stacked}, t} = R_{\text{EFA}, t} + \sum_{i=1}^{16} w_{i, t-1} \, r^{\text{xs}}_{i,t} - s_{\text{fin}} - c_{\text{tc}} \sum_{i=1}^{16} \lvert w_{i,t} - w_{i, t-1} \rvert
$$

Our baseline simulation charges an institutional **40 bps/year equity futures financing spread** ($s_{\text{fin}}$) and **5 bps one-way** (10 bps round-trip) slippage on weekly Friday rebalances, enforcing a strict **1-day execution lag** (close-$t$ signals earn day-$t+1$ returns).

### Signal engines: systematic trend vs. global macro

We prototype and evaluate two causal bottom-up signal families across all 16 contracts using fixed economic sign priors and zero in-sample parameter fitting:

1. **Systematic Trend (Multi-Horizon EWMA Crossover):** On each asset's cumulative log excess-return index $p_{i,t}$, we blend fast (`16/64` days, 25% weight), medium (`32/128` days, 40% weight), and slow (`64/256` days, 35% weight) exponentially weighted moving-average crossovers scaled by 42-day EWMA realized volatility $\sigma_{i,t}$ and bounded smoothly in $[-1, +1]$:

$$
S^{\text{trend}}_{i,t} = \sum_{(f,s)} \omega_{f,s} \, \tanh\!\left(\frac{\text{EWMA}_f(p_{i,t}) - \text{EWMA}_s(p_{i,t})}{1.2 \, \sigma_{i,t} \sqrt{s/2}}\right)
$$

2. **Global Macro (Growth, Inflation, Yield-Curve, and Implied FX Carry):** Four causal cross-asset macro signals capture economic regimes and carry before pure price trends turn:
   - **Growth pulse:** 63-day risk-adjusted changes in the Copper/Gold ratio (`HG=F / GC=F`) and high-yield credit spread proxy (`HYG / IEF`). Rising growth tilts long equities, cyclical FX (`FXE`, `FXB`, `FXA`), and industrial/energy commodities (`USO`, `DBA`), while tilting short safe-haven `FXY`.
   - **Inflation pulse:** 63-day breakeven inflation impulse (`TIP / IEF`) blended with 126-day broad commodity momentum (`DBC`). Rising inflation tilts long commodities (`GLD`, `SLV`, `USO`, `DBA`) and short sovereign duration (`SHY`, `IEF`, `TLT`, `BWX`).
   - **Yield-curve carry and policy trend:** The 10Y–3M Treasury slope (`^TNX - ^IRX`) rewards long duration when steep (positive carry) and penalizes duration when inverted, paired with 126-day 10Y yield momentum (`^TNX`).
   - **Implied FX carry:** Comparing each CurrencyShares ETF's 6-month total return against spot FX (`EURUSD=X`, `JPY=X`, `GBPUSD=X`, `AUDUSD=X`) and `BIL` extracts the implied foreign-minus-USD deposit rate differential directly from Yahoo Finance prices.

3. **Two-stage risk-parity volatility targeting:** Each of the four asset classes receives an **equal 25% risk budget** via inverse-volatility asset sizing (with annualized volatility floored at 2.5% to bound short-rate leverage in `SHY`). A causal 42-day portfolio volatility scalar then targets **10% annualized overlay volatility**, capped at **4.0× gross notional leverage**.

## Quantifying the catch: double-hit months and financing drag

Across **217 calendar months** from November 2008 to November 2026, `EFA` was negative in 86 months—and the overlay *also* lost money in **47 of those months** (**21.7%** of all months; **54.7%** of `EFA` down months). The six worst double-hit months show how stacked leverage concentrates risk during sudden liquidity reversals:

| Double-Hit Month | 100% EFA Return | Overlay Excess Return | Stacked Monthly Return | Macro Catalyst |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| **January 2009** | -13.73% | -2.14% | **-15.48%** | Post-Lehman bank solvency shock & Treasury reversal |
| **May 2010** | -11.19% | -2.07% | **-13.03%** | Greek debt crisis & Flash Crash risk-off spike |
| **September 2011** | -10.81% | -1.49% | **-12.06%** | Eurozone periphery contagion & gold liquidation |
| **May 2012** | -11.14% | -0.51% | **-11.61%** | Grexit fears & sharp commodity sell-off |
| **January 2014** | -5.20% | -5.64% | **-10.71%** | EM FX taper tantrum & surprise UST rally vs. short bond trend |
| **March 2026** | -7.83% | -2.50% | **-10.17%** | Cross-asset rate/equity whipsaw during policy repricing |

Financing spreads and slippage form the second structural hurdle. Because the overlay earns ~3.46%/yr net excess return before financing spreads, wide swap spreads or retail margin borrowing rapidly compress net alpha:

| Equity Futures Financing Spread | 3 bps / side TC (CAGR / Sharpe) | 5 bps / side TC (CAGR / Sharpe) | 10 bps / side TC (CAGR / Sharpe) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **0 bps / yr** (Idealized zero-spread futures) | 11.86% / 0.56 | 11.31% / 0.54 | 9.96% / 0.48 |
| **40 bps / yr** (Institutional futures / ETF baseline) | 11.41% / 0.54 | **10.87% / 0.52** | 9.52% / 0.47 |
| **80 bps / yr** (Wide swap / roll financing) | 10.97% / 0.53 | 10.43% / 0.50 | 9.08% / 0.45 |
| **120 bps / yr** (Retail margin / expensive wrapper) | 10.52% / 0.51 | 9.99% / 0.48 | 8.65% / 0.43 |

Even at punitive **120 bps/yr financing + 10 bps/side slippage**, the stacked portfolio (**8.65% CAGR**, **0.43 Sharpe**) still edges past unstacked `EFA` (**7.52%**, **0.40**)—but nearly two-thirds of the excess compounding disappears. Tight institutional futures financing is non-negotiable.

## Reference Python prototype and paper-trading architecture

Below is the core `yfinance` signal and volatility-targeting engine alongside the contract-sizing, margin-buffer, and `ib_async` paper-execution bridge required to move from historical research into live futures simulation. The full runnable script and daily backtest CSV are attached below.

```python
from dataclasses import dataclass
from typing import Dict, List
import numpy as np
import pandas as pd

ASSET_CLASSES: Dict[str, List[str]] = {
    "Equities": ["SPY", "VGK", "EWJ", "EEM"],
    "Rates": ["SHY", "IEF", "TLT", "BWX"],
    "FX": ["FXE", "FXY", "FXB", "FXA"],
    "Commodities": ["GLD", "SLV", "USO", "DBA"],
}
OVERLAY = [t for lst in ASSET_CLASSES.values() for t in lst]


@dataclass
class PortableAlphaConfig:
    capital: float = 100_000.0
    equity_notional_target: float = 100_000.0   # 1.0x international equity beta
    overlay_target_vol: float = 0.10            # 10% annualized overlay volatility
    trend_share: float = 0.60                   # 60% EWMA trend / 40% global macro
    top_down_blend: float = 0.30                # 30% top-down Ridge / 70% bottom-up
    max_gross_leverage: float = 4.0             # Hard cap on overlay gross notional
    min_cash_buffer_pct: float = 0.50           # Keep >= 50% capital in T-bills/cash


def build_signals(df: pd.DataFrame, raw_nyse: pd.DataFrame):
    """Causal EWMA trend + global macro signals on unfunded excess returns."""
    rets = df.pct_change().fillna(0.0)
    xs = rets[OVERLAY].sub(rets["BIL"], axis=0)
    vol_ann = (xs.ewm(span=42, min_periods=20).std() * np.sqrt(252)).clip(lower=0.025)
    log_px = np.log((1.0 + xs).cumprod())

    # 1) Multi-horizon EWMA crossover trend
    sig_trend = pd.DataFrame(0.0, index=df.index, columns=OVERLAY)
    for (fast, slow), w in [((16, 64), 0.25), ((32, 128), 0.40), ((64, 256), 0.35)]:
        diff = log_px.ewm(span=fast).mean() - log_px.ewm(span=slow).mean()
        sig_trend += w * np.tanh(diff / (1.2 * (vol_ann / np.sqrt(252)) * np.sqrt(slow / 2.0)))

    # 2) Causal global macro (Growth, Inflation, Yield-Curve, Implied FX Carry)
    cu_au = np.log(df["HG=F"] / df["GC=F"])
    hy_ief = np.log(df["HYG"] / df["IEF"])
    growth = (
        0.5 * (cu_au.diff(63) / (cu_au.diff().ewm(span=63).std() * np.sqrt(63))).clip(-3, 3)
        + 0.5 * (hy_ief.diff(63) / (hy_ief.diff().ewm(span=63).std() * np.sqrt(63))).clip(-3, 3)
    )
    tip_ief = np.log(df["TIP"] / df["IEF"])
    dbc_lp = np.log(df["DBC"])
    infl = (
        0.5 * (tip_ief.diff(63) / (tip_ief.diff().ewm(span=63).std() * np.sqrt(63))).clip(-3, 3)
        + 0.5 * (dbc_lp.diff(126) / (dbc_lp.diff().ewm(span=63).std() * np.sqrt(126))).clip(-3, 3)
    )
    curve_carry = np.tanh(((df["^TNX"] - df["^IRX"]) - 0.50) / 1.50)
    tnx_chg = (df["^TNX"].diff(126) / (df["^TNX"].diff().ewm(span=63).std() * np.sqrt(126))).clip(-3, 3)

    sig_macro = pd.DataFrame(0.0, index=df.index, columns=OVERLAY)
    for t in ASSET_CLASSES["Equities"]:
        sig_macro[t] = np.tanh((0.65 * growth - 0.35 * tnx_chg) / 1.2)
    for t in ASSET_CLASSES["Rates"]:
        sig_macro[t] = np.tanh((0.30 * curve_carry - 0.35 * infl - 0.35 * tnx_chg) / 1.1)
    for t in ["GLD", "SLV"]:
        sig_macro[t] = np.tanh((0.55 * infl - 0.45 * tnx_chg) / 1.1)
    for t in ["USO", "DBA"]:
        sig_macro[t] = np.tanh((0.60 * infl + 0.40 * growth) / 1.1)
    return xs, vol_ann, sig_trend, sig_macro


def size_futures_contracts(
    target_weights: pd.Series,
    capital: float,
    latest_prices: pd.Series,
    multipliers: Dict[str, float],
    fx_to_usd: Dict[str, float],
    initial_margin_used: float,
    cfg: PortableAlphaConfig,
) -> Dict[str, float]:
    """Translate risk-targeted weights into contract counts with a >= 50% cash margin guard."""
    if initial_margin_used / capital > (1.0 - cfg.min_cash_buffer_pct):
        raise RuntimeError("Margin buffer breach: cut overlay risk before trading.")
    return {
        mkt: (float(w) * capital) / (float(latest_prices[mkt]) * multipliers[mkt] * fx_to_usd.get(mkt, 1.0))
        for mkt, w in target_weights.items()
    }
```

When transitioning from `yfinance` prototyping to paper trading (for example via `ib_async` connected to IBKR TWS port `7497` or IB Gateway port `4002`), keep three operational safeguards active: enforce at least a **50% unencumbered T-bill/cash buffer** for variation margin spikes, trade on **weekly or 5% drift-band schedules** to cap roll/commission drag, and map the 16 ETF proxies onto liquid CME/ICE/Eurex micro or mini futures (`MES`, `M2K`, `ZN`, `ZT`, `6E`, `6J`, `MGC`, `MCL`) scaled by contract multipliers.

## Execution risks and what would change our view

<Callout title="Key implementation and behavioral risks" tone="risk">
  **Tracking error and behavioral abandonment are the biggest killers of portable alpha.** Even a strategy that compounds +335 bps/yr ahead of `EFA` over 18 years endured multi-year stretches—such as 2009–2012 (-333 bps/yr lag) and 2023–2026 (-191 bps/yr lag)—where the overlay dragged behind unleveraged equities while adding headline volatility. Investors who allocate after a blowout year like 2022 and capitulate during a choppy whipsaw year like 2026 lock in the exact opposite of the full-cycle compounding benefit.
</Callout>

Three observable shifts would materially alter our assessment of this strategy:

- **Persistent futures basis widening (adverse):** If dealer balance-sheet constraints push international equity futures roll spreads consistently above **100 bps/yr** over T-bills, the net risk-adjusted benefit of stacking compresses sharply relative to a lower-leverage allocation.
- **Expanded futures breadth beyond 16 ETF proxies (constructive):** Moving from 16 ETF proxies to 27–50 institutional futures contracts—adding natural gas, copper, grains, softs, European/Japanese sovereign bonds, and emerging FX directly—should deepen cross-sectional diversification and trim standalone overlay drawdowns below -15%.
- **Structural persistence of positive stock-bond correlation (constructive):** Whenever inflation volatility remains elevated and sovereign bonds correlate positively with equities (as in 2022), traditional `60/40` portfolios lose their primary shock absorber, increasing the portfolio value of a multi-asset trend and global macro overlay.
