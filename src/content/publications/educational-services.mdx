## Baseline strategy architecture

Our baseline model combines two market-neutral cross-sectional signals evaluated daily near the US close (**15:55 ET Limit-on-Close**), targeting holding horizons of **3 to 15 trading days** without look-ahead bias (signals formed from day $t$ closes execute against day $t+1$ realized USD returns).

### Currency normalization and leave-one-out peer benchmark

Before computing signals, we strip daily sterling moves $R_{\text{GBPUSD}, t}$ from the NYSE ADR returns of `RELX` and `PSO` so cross-sectional comparisons reflect local equity repricing:

$$
R^{\text{loc}}_{i,t} = \frac{1 + R^{\text{USD}}_{i,t}}{1 + R_{\text{GBPUSD}, t}} - 1, \quad i \in \{\text{RELX}, \text{PSO}\}
$$

Rather than regressing a stock on a sector index that contains its own return—which biases $\beta_{\text{RELX}}$ upward given `RELX`'s 45% base weight—we construct a **leave-one-out liquidity-weighted peer factor** $R_{\text{peer}, -i, t}$ from base weights $w^{\text{bmk}} = \{0.45\text{ RELX}, 0.30\text{ PSO}, 0.15\text{ WLY}, 0.10\text{ MH}\}$, excluding `SCHL` from benchmark construction to prevent small-cap noise contamination and renormalizing dynamically prior to `MH`'s July 24, 2025 listing:

$$
R_{\text{peer}, -i, t} = \frac{\sum_{j \neq i, \, j \notin \{\text{SCHL}\}} w^{\text{bmk}}_j \, R^{\text{loc}}_{j,t}}{\sum_{j \neq i, \, j \notin \{\text{SCHL}\}} w^{\text{bmk}}_j}
$$

### Signal A: Beta-adjusted residual mean reversion

Over a trailing 60-trading-day OLS window, we decompose each stock's local return into peer-sector sensitivity, broad market (`SPY`) sensitivity, and idiosyncratic residual $\epsilon_{i,t}$:

$$
R^{\text{loc}}_{i,t} = \alpha_i + \beta_{i,\text{peer}} R_{\text{peer}, -i, t} + \beta_{i,\text{SPY}} R_{\text{SPY}, t} + \epsilon_{i,t}
$$

We accumulate the 5-day idiosyncratic residual and standardize by its trailing 20-day standard deviation $\sigma_{\epsilon, i, 20}$:

$$
Z^{\text{MR}}_{i,t} = -\frac{\sum_{s=0}^{4} \epsilon_{i, t-s}}{\sigma_{\epsilon, i, 20}}
$$

When `WLY` or `MH` sells off over a trading week without a commensurate drop in `RELX`, `PSO`, or `SPY`—and outside an earnings window—$Z^{\text{MR}}_{i,t} > 0$ flags a temporary liquidity dislocation.

### Signal B: Institutional lead-lag drift

Institutional capital reprices sector-wide catalysts (such as academic AI corpus licensing deals or interest-rate shifts) first in `RELX` and `PSO`. We measure the 3-day volatility-normalized impulse of the two liquid leaders:

$$
L_t = \frac{1}{2} \left( \frac{R^{\text{3d}}_{\text{RELX}, t}}{\sigma_{\text{RELX}, 20}} + \frac{R^{\text{3d}}_{\text{PSO}, t}}{\sigma_{\text{PSO}, 20}} \right)
$$

For the three smaller names $i \in \{\text{WLY}, \text{MH}, \text{SCHL}\}$, the laggard catch-up score measures the normalized gap relative to the leaders (setting $Z^{\text{LL}}_{i,t} = 0$ for `RELX` and `PSO`):

$$
Z^{\text{LL}}_{i,t} = L_t - \frac{R^{\text{3d}}_{i, t}}{\sigma_{i, 20}}
$$

### Signal combination, event filters, and risk constraints

We form a 60/40 composite score $S_{i,t} = 0.60 \, Z^{\text{MR}}_{i,t} + 0.40 \, Z^{\text{LL}}_{i,t}$, subject to three risk guards and inverse-volatility sizing:

1. **Earnings blackout:** Flatten stock $i$ ($w_{i,t} = 0$) from `T-2` trading days before through `T+1` trading days after quarterly or half-year earnings announcements (merging NYSE ADR calendars with LSE announcements for `REL.L` and `PSON.L`).
2. **News-shock circuit breaker:** Whenever $\lvert R^{\text{loc}}_{i,t} \rvert > 3.5 \, \sigma_{i,20}$ on a single day—typical of M&A rumors, activist filings, or guidance resets—freeze entries in stock $i$ for 5 trading days to avoid fading structural repricings.
3. **Dollar-neutral inverse-volatility weights:** Cross-sectionally demean active scores $\tilde{S}_{i,t} = S_{i,t} - \bar{S}_t$, scale by $1/\sigma_{i,20}$, normalize to unit gross exposure ($\sum_i \lvert w_{i,t} \rvert = 1$), cap single-name exposure at **35%** (**15%** for `SCHL`), and hedge residual index beta with `SPY` whenever $\lvert \sum_i w_{i,t} \beta_{i,\text{SPY}} \rvert > 0.10$.

## Technical extensions: Kalman hedging and seasonal conditioning

Testing the baseline specification on 2021–2026 market data exposes three structural weaknesses in a naive rolling-OLS implementation:

- **OLS window lag during regime shifts:** When `WLY` signed generative-AI training-data agreements in 2024–2025 and when `MH` entered price discovery following its July 2025 IPO, 60-day unweighted OLS betas lagged true factor sensitivities by several weeks.
- **Unconditional `SCHL` noise:** Trading `SCHL` as a sector mean-reversion candidate when its 60-day peer correlation drops near zero injects unhedged consumer/retail book-fair risk at a steep **30 bps** round-trip spread cost.
- **Excessive daily churn:** A narrow 3% rebalance buffer triggers **141x–156x annualized turnover**, allowing mid/small-cap bid-ask spreads to consume more than 100% of gross alpha.

We address all three defects with two model extensions and a hysteresis execution rule.

### State-space Kalman filter dynamic hedging

We replace the 60-day rolling OLS regression with an online linear Gaussian state-space model in which stock $i$'s time-varying parameter vector $\theta_{i,t} = [\alpha_{i,t}, \, \beta_{i,\text{peer},t}, \, \beta_{i,\text{mkt},t}]^\top$ evolves as a random walk, pairing `SCHL` with the Russell 2000 (`IWM`) rather than `SPY` to isolate small-cap factor exposure:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\theta_{i,t} &= \theta_{i,t-1} + \eta_{i,t}, \quad \eta_{i,t} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, Q) \\
R^{\text{loc}}_{i,t} &= F_{i,t}^\top \theta_{i,t} + \nu_{i,t}, \quad \nu_{i,t} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, R_{i,t})
\end{aligned}
$$

Here $F_{i,t} = [1, \, R_{\text{peer}, -i, t}, \, R_{\text{mkt}, t}]^\top$, state drift covariance is set to $Q = \text{diag}(10^{-7}, \, 1.5 \times 10^{-4}, \, 1.5 \times 10^{-4})$ so intercept drift stays anchored near zero while hedge ratios adapt smoothly, and observation variance $R_{i,t}$ updates recursively via an EWMA ($\lambda = 0.96$) of one-step-ahead pre-fit innovations $e_{i,t} = R^{\text{loc}}_{i,t} - F_{i,t}^\top \hat{\theta}_{i, t\mid t-1}$:

$$
K_{i,t} = \frac{P_{i, t\mid t-1} F_{i,t}}{F_{i,t}^\top P_{i, t\mid t-1} F_{i,t} + R_{i,t}}, \quad \hat{\theta}_{i, t\mid t} = \hat{\theta}_{i, t\mid t-1} + K_{i,t} e_{i,t}
$$

Using the causal pre-fit innovation $e_{i,t}$ in place of static OLS residuals eliminates look-ahead bias while adapting immediately to post-IPO and AI-licensing beta shifts.

### Calendar seasonality filter and peer-correlation gate

To align signal conviction with institutional calendar cycles and cross-sectional breadth, we apply three structural conditioners prior to portfolio construction:

1. **`SCHL` correlation decoupling gate:** Compute the trailing 60-day correlation $\rho_{60}(\text{SCHL}, R_{\text{peer}})$. Whenever $\rho_{60} < 0.15$, set $S_{\text{SCHL}, t} = 0$ so the book does not trade `SCHL` against B2B publishers during decoupled retail regimes.
2. **Book-fair and adoption seasonality:** Attenuate `SCHL` composite scores (0.35× multiplier) during peak book-fair and fiscal jump months (July, September, December), and scale down contrarian short signals (0.50× multiplier) on `MH` and `PSO` during the August–November fall semester billing window.
3. **Hysteresis entry and 12% no-trade band:** Require cross-sectionally demeaned signal dispersion $\max_i \lvert \tilde{S}_{i,t} \rvert \ge 0.80$ to initiate a fresh book, hold active positions until their score crosses zero, and rebalance existing weights only when $\lvert w^{\text{target}}_{i,t} - w^{\text{curr}}_{i,t} \rvert > 0.12$.

### Signal decay across holding horizons

Testing forward cross-sectional spread returns across holding horizons from 1 to 20 trading days explains why turnover discipline must preserve short-horizon responsiveness:

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1 day** | +2.4 bps | +1.5 bps (+4.3 bps post-MH) | +3.1 bps | Initial liquidity bounce and leader propagation |

| **5 days** | -9.7 bps | -3.6 bps | -6.2 bps | Lead-lag impulse exhausted; fundamental drift dominates |

Using hysteresis thresholds ($\lvert \tilde{S} \rvert \ge 0.80$ entry, zero-crossing exit, 12% no-trade band) captures the initial impulse without churning positions daily.

## Reference Python implementation

Below is the core state-space Kalman filter and seasonal portfolio construction loop used to generate the empirical results above. The complete runnable script and daily backtest series are attached underneath.

```python
import numpy as np
import pandas as pd

def kalman_dynamic_residuals(
    rets_loc: pd.DataFrame,
    loo_peer: dict[str, pd.Series],
    spy_ret: pd.Series,
    iwm_ret: pd.Series,
) -> tuple[pd.DataFrame, pd.DataFrame, pd.DataFrame]:
    """Causal 1-step-ahead Kalman innovations and time-varying factor betas."""
    idx, stocks = rets_loc.index, list(rets_loc.columns)
    eps = pd.DataFrame(np.nan, index=idx, columns=stocks)
    beta_spy = pd.DataFrame(0.0, index=idx, columns=stocks)
    beta_iwm = pd.DataFrame(0.0, index=idx, columns=stocks)

    for s in stocks:
        mkt = iwm_ret.values if s == "SCHL" else spy_ret.values
        y, x_peer = rets_loc[s].values, loo_peer[s].values
        theta = np.array([0.0, 0.5, 0.5])
        P = np.eye(3) * 0.20
        Q = np.diag([1e-7, 1.5e-4, 1.5e-4])
        R_var, active = 0.02**2, 0

        for t in range(1, len(idx)):
            if np.isnan(y[t]) or np.isnan(x_peer[t]) or np.isnan(mkt[t]):
                continue
            active += 1
            F = np.array([1.0, x_peer[t], mkt[t]])
            P_pred = P + Q
            innov = y[t] - float(F @ theta)          # pre-fit innovation (causal)
            S_var = float(F @ P_pred @ F.T) + R_var
            K = (P_pred @ F) / S_var
            theta = theta + K * innov
            P = (np.eye(3) - np.outer(K, F)) @ P_pred
            R_var = 0.96 * R_var + 0.04 * (innov**2)

            if active >= 30:
                eps.iat[t, eps.columns.get_loc(s)] = innov
                if s == "SCHL":
                    beta_iwm.iat[t, beta_iwm.columns.get_loc(s)] = theta[2]
                    beta_spy.iat[t, beta_spy.columns.get_loc(s)] = theta[1] * 0.32
                else:
                    beta_spy.iat[t, beta_spy.columns.get_loc(s)] = theta[2] + theta[1] * 0.32

    return eps, beta_spy, beta_iwm
```

## Execution risks and what would change our view

<Callout title="Live deployment constraints" tone="risk">
  **Small-breadth and borrow-fee sensitivity:** Five stocks sit at the absolute lower bound of cross-sectional statistical arbitrage. If M&A, privatization, or prolonged earnings blackouts shrink the active book below four names—as occurred prior to `MH`'s July 2025 listing—spread costs overwhelm gross alpha. In addition, `MH` (\$7.6M ADV, 60.1% realized vol) and `SCHL` (\$0.68B cap) can experience episodic short-borrow fee spikes around Q3/Q4 adoption announcements, and 15:55 ET Limit-on-Close signals approximate 16:00 ET closing auction clears to within intraday auction imbalance slippage.
</Callout>

Three developments would materially alter our assessment of this strategy:

- **Universe expansion (constructive):** Adding liquid adjacent information-services and ed-tech peers—such as Wolters Kluwer (`WKL.AS`), Informa (`INF.L`), Coursera (`COUR`), or Stride (`LRN`)—would raise cross-sectional breadth from 5 to 8–10 names, stabilizing demeaned z-scores and reducing leader-shorting drag on `RELX` and `PSO`.
- **Asymmetric ETF funding (constructive):** Replacing internal `RELX`/`PSO` short legs during strong positive leader impulses ($L_t > 1.5$) with a beta-matched `SPY`/`IWM` hedge would preserve follower catch-up alpha in `WLY` and `MH` without stepping in front of institutional momentum in `RELX`.
- **Wider post-close auction spreads (adverse):** If realized round-trip execution slippage in `WLY`, `MH`, and `SCHL` exceeds **1.75×** our modeled baseline (above 22 bps in `WLY`/`MH` and above 50 bps in `SCHL`), net Sharpe in the 5-stock regime compresses toward zero, confining these signals to execution-timing overlays for a fundamental book rather than standalone deployment.
