Arizona summers are a stress test most grids never face: triple-digit heat, evening solar ramp-down, and air conditioners that all switch on at once. Here is what the desert grid gets right about storage — and what it means for our coverage.

## Lesson 1: duration beats power

Most headlines quote megawatts. Operators care about **megawatt-hours**. A 100 MW battery with two hours of storage covers the evening ramp; the same 100 MW with thirty minutes of storage does not.

![Illustrative chart of rising data center power demand](/publications/grid-demand.svg)

| Duration | Covers | Desert verdict |
| -------- | ------ | -------------- |
| 30 min | Frequency response | Useful, not sufficient |
| 2–4 h | Evening ramp | The workhorse |
| 8 h+ | Overnight load | Rare, expensive, watch this space |

<Callout title="Takeaway" tone="note">
  When you read a storage announcement, divide the MWh by the MW first. Duration is the spec that matters.
</Callout>

## Lesson 2: heat derates everything

Nameplate capacity assumes lab conditions. At 45°C, inverters throttle and cell degradation accelerates. Our back-of-the-envelope derating:

$$
P_{actual} = P_{nameplate} \times (1 - 0.004 \times (T - 25))
$$

At $T = 45$°C that is an 8% haircut before any other losses — material for project returns.

## What we are watching

1. **Long-duration pilots** in the Southwest — 8-hour chemistries moving from demo to PPA.
2. **Interconnection reform** — queue delays matter more than cell prices now.
3. **Monsoon-season performance data** — heat plus humidity is the real test.

Short post, simple point: cover the evening ramp first, and always read the duration spec.
