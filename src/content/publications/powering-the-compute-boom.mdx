## The short version

Hyperscalers need **firm, carbon-free megawatts** on five-year timelines. Wind and solar are cheap but intermittent. Batteries cover hours, not weeks. That leaves one scalable answer for baseload: **nuclear**, plus gas peakers as a bridge.

## Modeling the gap

We model load growth as compound demand from AI training and inference clusters, offset by grid retirements. In steady state, required firm capacity $C$ follows:

$$
C = \frac{D \times (1 + r)^t}{1 - m}
$$

where $D$ is current peak demand, $r$ is the annual growth rate, $t$ is years, and $m$ is the retirement margin. At $r = 9\%$ and $t = 6$, the arithmetic gets uncomfortable fast.

## How we sized the shortfall

The screening script is intentionally simple — clone it, point it at real EIA tables, and extend:

```python
def shortfall(demand_gw: float, firm_gw: float, margin: float = 0.15) -> float:
    """Return GW of firm capacity still needed at target reserve margin."""
    required = demand_gw * (1 + margin)
    return max(0.0, required - firm_gw)


print(shortfall(demand_gw=92.0, firm_gw=68.0))
# -> 37.8
```
