## Same label, three different machines

All three get called emerging-market fintech. The label hides the product.

Nubank is a bank. It takes deposits, it lends, and it earns a spread, plus fees. In the third quarter it had 142.6 million customers: nearly 120 million in Brazil, 16.6 million in Mexico, and 6.1 million in Colombia. Monthly revenue per active customer was \$17.70 and the monthly cost to serve that customer was about \$1.10. The distance between those two numbers is the franchise. Credit losses are what can close it.

Kaspi is a super-app that happens to own a bank. Payments, a loan book, and a marketplace sit in one product, used hard: the company reports 77 transactions a month per active consumer, across more than 27 million consumers and 907 thousand merchants in Kazakhstan and Türkiye. Profit today is still a Kazakh story. Türkiye, through a 93.5% stake in Hepsiburada, is the option and the drag.

dLocal is a switch. A global merchant — a streaming service, a ride-hailing app, an advertiser — uses it to collect or pay out in markets where that merchant does not want a local stack. dLocal does not have 140 million retail customers. It has volume. Third-quarter total payment volume was \$18.3 billion, up 77% from a year earlier. The equity question is what it keeps out of each of those dollars.

## Nubank is the one I would own

Gross revenue in the third quarter was \$6.26 billion. The company calls that up 37% year over year on an FX-neutral basis. Set against the \$4.32 billion it reported for the third quarter of 2025, the dollar increase is about 45%. Net income was \$1.09 billion, up 31% on the company's FX-neutral basis and about 39% in dollars versus the \$783 million reported a year earlier. Annualized return on equity was 32%. Net interest margin was 22.5%. After credit costs, the reported risk-adjusted margin was 12.1%, down from 12.4% in the second quarter; excluding items the company called non-recurring, it rose to 12.7% from 11.9%.

The customer curve is still rising, and it is no longer the whole story. Nubank ended 2023 with 93.9 million customers, 2024 with 114.2 million, and 2025 with 131 million. It added 3.7 million in the latest quarter. In Brazil it already serves 65% of adults, and the activity rate there has risen for seven straight quarters to 86.3%. Further Brazilian growth has to come from doing more with people it already has. That is happening. Dollar ARPAC in the company's releases went from \$10.60 in the fourth quarter of 2023 to \$15 in the fourth quarter of 2025 and \$17.70 in the third quarter of 2026. The 2025 release put the year-over-year ARPAC increase at about 27% on an FX-neutral basis, so this is not only a currency effect.

Full-year net income was about \$1.0 billion in 2023, \$1.97 billion in 2024, and \$2.9 billion in 2025. The second quarter of 2026 was the first single quarter above \$1 billion, at \$1.06 billion. The third quarter was \$1.09 billion. If that third quarter simply repeated, annual earnings would be about \$4.4 billion and the \$57 billion market cap would be about 13 times that pace. I am not forecasting the repeat. A data vendor's forward multiple, 11.7 times, says the consensus expects something at least that strong. I treat the forward multiple as the market's number, not mine.

A bank multiple is a statement about how long the return on equity lasts. A standard way to write it:

$$
\frac{P}{B} = \frac{ROE - g}{r - g}
$$

Nu's price-to-book on the same vendor page is 3.98. Suppose a dollar cost of equity of 14%, a return on equity that fades from today's 32% to 25%, and growth of 8%. That sketch is worth about 2.8 times book. The stock at 4 times book is therefore a bet that the return stays closer to the low thirties than to the mid-twenties, or that growth stays faster than 8% for longer than a fade assumes. Both are assumptions I am making, not facts the company reported.[^gordon] I am willing to underwrite them while ARPAC is still rising and Mexico has only just become a bank. Mexico added 870 thousand customers in its first quarter with a banking license, reached 16.6 million people (17% of adults, up 27% year over year), and saw deposit insurance rise sixteenfold to about \$195,000 per customer. Deposits for the group were \$47.5 billion against a credit portfolio of \$41.6 billion, so the balance sheet is funded by customers, not by the wholesale market.

### The credit book is the risk that can break it

The early-delinquency ratio spent the last two year-ends at 4.1%. It was 4.8% in the second quarter of 2026 and 4.9% in the third. The 90-day-plus ratio was 6.6% at the end of 2025, 6.9% in the second quarter, and 7.3% in the third. Coverage of that 90-day book was 234%. On interest-earning balances the picture is mixed rather than simply worse: the 15-to-90-day ratio improved to 8.0% from 8.2% in the prior quarter, and the 90-day ratio on that basis rose to 12.1% from 11.6%.

Unsecured personal-loan originations in Brazil were a record R\$22.1 billion, up 28% year over year. Management is leaning into a higher-yield book at the same time the stock of late loans is rising. A 32% return on equity can be what a credit cycle looks like near its best. The share price is down about 26% over the past 52 weeks while those earnings were being delivered, which is why the multiple is tolerable. I would rather buy that drawdown than buy the same bank at a peak multiple and a 4.1% early-delinquency print. The buy is the franchise at this price. It is not a claim that Brazilian consumer credit has finished getting worse.

## Kaspi is getting paid to wait

Kaspi's third-quarter revenue was KZT 1.12 trillion, which the company translates as \$2.5 billion. On a constant-currency basis that is up 18%. As reported, in tenge, it is about flat: KZT 1,117 billion against KZT 1,110 billion a year earlier. The company attributes the gap to the Turkish lira, which fell 32% against the tenge over the year to September, and notes that the dollar fell 20% against the tenge over the same span. Net income was KZT 281 billion, \$637 million, up 1% in tenge. Nine-month net income was \$1.80 billion, and nine-month growth in net income was zero.

So the cheap multiple is not a secret the market has missed. Profit has stopped growing. Adjusted EBITDA was up 1%, to KZT 408 billion (\$925 million), and the company's guidance for 2026 is about 5% growth in consolidated adjusted EBITDA. Marketplace constant-currency GMV guidance was revised down to about 12%. Anyone buying Kaspi for a reacceleration this year is buying a story the company is not telling.

What the company is telling is a mix story. Constant-currency marketplace revenue grew 20%, to KZT 466 billion, ahead of constant-currency GMV growth of 8%. Inside that, e-commerce constant-currency revenue grew 23% against GMV growth of 17%, and the third-party e-commerce take rate rose 2.1 percentage points to 16.3%. Purchases per e-commerce consumer rose from 12.8 to 16.3. In Kazakhstan, items sold rose 24% while the average selling price fell 8%, because a stronger tenge made imports cheaper and merchants passed that through. Unit growth is healthier than the GMV print. e-Grocery GMV rose 40%.

The profit pool is the loan book, not the marketplace.

Fintech revenue grew 19%, on a 14% increase in the average net loan portfolio to KZT 7.5 trillion (\$17.1 billion). The company said the portfolio yield was stable at 6%, and that it is deliberately lengthening the book: average duration moved from 7.9 months to 9.1. Revenue of KZT 489 billion on a KZT 7.5 trillion average book is about 6.5% of the book in a single quarter, and that revenue is broader than pure interest.[^kaspiyield] Funding is what ate the growth. Fintech adjusted EBITDA fell 1% even as revenue rose 19%, because deposits, and the rate paid on them, grew faster than the loan book. Savings balances grew 21%. The three-month deposit, about a third of deposits, was cut by 100 basis points in August and another 100 basis points in October. Management expects a modest benefit in the fourth quarter and a clearer one in 2027.

### The dividend is the bull case

The board has proposed another quarterly dividend of KZT 1,000 per ADS. The release does not translate it. Using the company's own third-quarter translation — KZT 281 billion of net income equals \$637 million — gives 441.1 tenge per dollar. At that rate, KZT 1,000 is \$2.267. Four such quarters are \$9.07, which is 10.4% of the \$86.87 close.

| Step | Figure | How it is calculated |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Q3 net income | KZT 281 billion = \$637 million | Company translation |
| Tenge per dollar | 441.1 | 281,000 ÷ 637 |
| Quarterly dividend | KZT 1,000 per ADS | Board proposal |
| In dollars, at that rate | \$2.267 | 1,000 ÷ 441.1 |
| Four quarters | \$9.07 | \$2.267 × 4, if the proposal is repeated |
| Yield on the 23 Nov close | 10.4% | \$9.07 ÷ \$86.87 |

That 10% is an indicated yield under three conditions: shareholders approve the proposal, the board keeps proposing it, and the tenge stays near the rate embedded in the third-quarter dollar figures. It is not the cash already paid. The same market-data page that supplied the \$86.87 close shows a trailing dividend of \$3.92, which is about a 4.5% yield. The dividend has been stepping up. I am underwriting the step-up only as a proposal.

The nine-month profit of \$1.80 billion is a \$2.4 billion annual pace. Against a \$16.5 billion market cap, that is about 7 times earnings. You are being offered most of the earnings back in cash, at a proposed double-digit yield, for a business whose constant-currency revenue grew 18% and whose reported profit did not grow at all. The share price is up about 22% over the past year, and the market cap is still about 37% under the July 2024 high the same data page records. This is a stalled compounder at a low multiple, not a collapse.

Türkiye is why I size it behind Nubank. Hepsiburada is around breakeven on the company's description, order frequency there rose 11%, and a new shopping-loan product, Hepsicredit, financed about 8% of smartphone GMV in October. Management expects investment in Türkiye to moderate significantly in 2027. I will believe that when the consolidated profit grows. Until then the Kazakh engine is funding a second country.

Credit is the other restraint. The NPL ratio was 6.9% for the first nine months of 2026, against 6.1% for the full year 2025, though it eased slightly from the second quarter. The company says coverage is lower because the mix is moving toward car loans and merchant loans and away from buy-now-pay-later, which it regards as lower risk. A safer mix should pull the ratio down. The ratio is up year over year anyway. I do not know how much of that is mix math versus a weaker borrower. It is enough to keep the position smaller than Nubank.

Payments are the quiet good business underneath: constant-currency TPV up 14% to KZT 13.6 trillion (\$30.8 billion), revenue up 6% to KZT 182 billion, take rate 1.01% versus 1.03% a year ago. Adjusted EBITDA there was roughly flat, partly on technology spend the company ties to Kaspi Alaqan. I do not need payments to be the upside. I need them to keep the daily habit that makes the loan book and the marketplace hard to leave.

## dLocal can wait

dLocal's quarter was strong on every headline that is a flow, and uncomfortable on every headline that is a rate.

Volume was \$18.3 billion, up 77% year over year and 68% in constant currency, the eighth straight quarter above 50% and the fourth at or above 70%. Net revenue retention was 140%. Revenue was \$398 million, up 41%, and almost unchanged from \$399.7 million in the second quarter. Investing.com reported that the print was short of a \$406.7 million consensus. Gross profit was a record \$132 million, up 28%, and up 4% from the prior quarter. Net income was a record \$68 million, up 31%. Operating profit was \$76 million, up 37%, and operating expenses fell 11% from the prior quarter. The operating-profit-to-gross-profit ratio reached 58%, from 50% in the second quarter and 54% a year earlier. Nine-month adjusted free cash flow was \$182 million. The company bought back 9.1 million shares for \$120.1 million through the end of the quarter. Dividing those two figures gives about \$13.20 a share, above the \$12.65 close.

If the note stopped there, dLocal would be a buy. The price of a unit of volume is why it is not.

The mix explains the direction. On the earnings call, local-to-local flow was 62% of volume, driven by ride-hailing and on-demand delivery, and payouts were 25%. Both earn less than a cross-border pay-in, because there is no currency conversion in the first and a thinner fee in the second. Large merchants also step into lower price tiers as they grow. Mexico was called out specifically: processing costs ran high, including catch-up costs from earlier periods, and a few large merchants sit in the lowest tiers. Management said that should not be a lasting problem. Brazil had a mix shift in payment methods, and social-media advertising there was strong on election spending that the company expects to fade.

> I wouldn't say we've hit the bottom in terms of take rate compression. But certainly, if you look sequentially, the declines are beginning to slow down as we anticipated they would.

Source: Pedro Arnt, chief executive, dLocal third-quarter 2026 earnings call, 12 November 2026.

I am taking him at his word on both halves. The decline is slowing. He is not prepared to say it is over. The flat 0.72% also came with help the company described and did not quantify: lower advancement costs in Argentina, a one-off cost recovery in Nigeria, and gains on FX hedges. A ratio that is unchanged only after those items is not yet a clean run-rate.

Guidance was left in the same ranges, with the weighting moved. Volume growth is tracking toward the top of the 60% to 70% range. Gross-profit growth is around the middle of 25% to 30%. Operating-profit growth is toward the bottom of 27.5% to 32.5%, with an estimated six-percentage-point swing in fourth-quarter year-over-year gross-profit growth from FX in Brazil, Mexico, and Argentina. Nine-month net income was \$165 million. Stretched over a year that is about \$220 million, and the \$3.64 billion market cap is about 17 times it, in line with the 17.1 trailing multiple. The vendor's forward multiple is 11.8. Corporate cash was \$537 million inside \$1.03 billion of total cash. The rest of the cash is the float of a payments business, matched by what dLocal owes merchants. I would not subtract the float and call the equity cheaper.

Insiders own about 49% of the company, on the same statistics page as the close. Alignment is real. It does not set the take rate. Merchants do, by sending more of the cheap volume.

A hold, said plainly: I would keep shares I already owned, and I would not start a position at \$12.65. The operating leverage in the quarter was genuine. The thing the multiple depends on, gross profit per dollar of volume, has had one flat quarter that included one-off benefits, and the chief executive will not call the bottom. One more clean quarter would change the call. This one does not.

## How I would size them

Among these three only, a fresh book would be about three fifths Nubank and two fifths Kaspi, and no dLocal. Nubank is the larger weight because profit is compounding and the drawdown did the multiple's work. Kaspi is the smaller weight because reported profit is flat, the loan-loss ratio is higher than last year, and Türkiye has not earned its keep yet. The dividend is why Kaspi is a buy at all. dLocal is the best operating momentum and the murkiest unit price, which is a bad mix for new money.

The 85 shares and 12 shares on the publication portfolio are a record of the two buys at roughly equal dollars, not this weighting. A real book would hold more Nubank than Kaspi per dollar. Neither position is a view on the real, the tenge, or the lira as a separate trade. All three equities are a currency bet whether or not the model mentions it. Nubank's dollar profit rose faster than its FX-neutral profit over the past year, which means the real helped. Kaspi's reported quarter was a tenge-versus-lira event as much as an operating event. dLocal already reports in dollars and still warned that fourth-quarter gross profit hinges on three exchange rates.

## What would change my mind

I would sell Nubank if the 90-day delinquency ratio pushed through 8% and the risk-adjusted net interest margin fell below 10% in the same quarter. I would also sell it if the return on equity settled in the high teens and the stock was still near 4 times book. A higher delinquency ratio on its own, of the size seen this quarter, is the risk I already accepted.

I would sell Kaspi if the NPL ratio made a new high above the 6.9% nine-month reading and the deposit-rate cuts failed to show up in 2027 funding costs. I would sell it if investment in Türkiye rose again after management said it would moderate. A single flat quarter of profit would not do it. Flat is the base I am buying.

I would buy dLocal after two further quarters in which gross profit stays at least 0.72% of volume, the company does not attribute the level to one-off cost recovery or hedge gains, and gross-profit dollars are still growing. I would sell dLocal if gross-profit dollars were flat or down for two quarters while volume was up. That is the case in which the company is processing more and earning less, in absolute money.

These are lines I would use. They are not valuations, and they will look arbitrary if the facts arrive in some other combination. The fourth-quarter releases are the next time they can be hit.
